# Student Applications

Please reach out with a brief description of what you're interested in, why you're interested in working with us, and include your CV and transcript.
Generally, it will be necessary that you're already enrolled at Cornell; for graduate-level students, note that you are welcome to reach out and discuss with faculty, but admission to Cornell is a separate procedure.

Please read our [Lab Culture] page before reaching out.
You can also see an (incomplete) list of open projects listed at [ug-meng-projects](https://ug-meng-projects.coecis.cornell.edu).

## Lab Culture and Expectations

We value transparency, openness, and active collaboration and communication.

### Preamble

Especially for students considering graduate school, please read _[Some Modest Advice for Graduate Students](https://polypedal.berkeley.edu/?page_id=266#advice)_, which captures most of what I would tell you better than I can express.

I also encourage you to watch _[Randy Pausch's Last Lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ji5_MqicxSo)_.

### Expectations

Research is largely about deciding the right **destination**, choosing the **path**, and then doing the **work** of actually walking the path.

For **Undegraduates**, we will set the destination and path, and expect you to take ownership of getting the work done. We will help you along the way, and give you advice and help to learn the tools you need, but you are responsible for actually walking the path and getting to the destination. More concretely, you can expect to:

- Learn some skills
- Learn to be part of a team
  - Be reliable
  - Communicate actively
- Pick up good engineering habits
  - Take pride in your work. 
  It’s not just about getting it to work; if another engineer looks under the hood, you want them to say “A professional has been at work here”.
  - Fix the cause, not the symptom. Understand why!
  You won’t always be able to chase down every weird anomaly, but as an engineer, that should bug you, like an itch you can’t reach.
